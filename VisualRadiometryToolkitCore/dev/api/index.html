API Reference

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore — Module
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore

The core of the Visual Radiometry Toolkit: the types of a radiometric camera, a camera of the Visual Geometry Toolkit (VisualGeometryToolkitCore) together with the parts that decide how much light a pixel collects, and of the scene's light. Radiometry lifts geometry: this package depends on VisualGeometryToolkitCore and follows its conventions; nothing there depends on this.

  • A camera's vignetting: NoVignetting (absent, V ≡ 1), the B-spline fields TensorProductVignetting and RadialVignetting, the log-domain LogVignetting, and a measured BinnedRadialProfile.
  • A camera's focus surface: Focus, the inverse focus distance along each chief ray as a thin plate spline.
  • The radiometric camera, one object: RadiometricCameraModel, a geometric camera with its focus surface, aperture radius and vignetting, and its back-projection from the aperture disc (backproject(camera, 𝐩, 𝐚), the paper's π̂⁻¹; world_ray).
  • The sensor's pixels and the quadrature rules of the image-formation integrals: SensorQuadrature on a pixel, ApertureQuadrature on the aperture.
  • The scene's light: an EnvironmentLight of a SphericalHarmonicMap.
  • A surface's reflectance: Material, a spatially varying Cook–Torrance BSDF with a GGX microfacet distribution.
  • A printed planar target: TexturedTarget, a posed planar target of VisualGeometryToolkitCore with its texture, its printed edge and its own state (TargetState: its warping, its printed edge, its in-plane print error).

The radiometric camera

A RadiometricCameraModel is one object: a geometric camera of VisualGeometryToolkitCore (geometric), a focus surface, an aperture radius and a vignetting. Composed from a geometric camera it has NoVignetting unless one is stated. Its camera-frame maps are the unit chief direction (chief_direction), the focus point focus_point on the Petzval surface (the paper's 𝔣(𝐩)) and the radiometric back-projection (the paper's π̂⁻¹(𝐩, 𝐚)), the direction of the ray from the aperture point 𝐚 to the focus point; world_ray is that ray at a pose. Each has one implementation, on the parts it reads, so a record that holds the same parts forwards to it.

The radiometric back-projection of a camera is a method of VisualGeometryToolkitCore's backproject, with the aperture point as a third argument: backproject(camera, 𝐩) is the geometric camera's chief ray, backproject(camera, 𝐩, 𝐚) the ray from the aperture point 𝐚. On a camera's parts it is aperture_direction.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.π̂⁻¹ — Method
backproject(camera::RadiometricCameraModel, 𝐩, 𝐚[, t̂]) -> SVector{3}

The radiometric back-projection, the paper's π̂⁻¹(𝐩, 𝐚) (public alias π̂⁻¹): the unit direction of the ray of the sensor position 𝐩 from the aperture point 𝐚 (camera frame), towards the focus point 𝔣(𝐩) — a method of VisualGeometryToolkitCore's backproject with the aperture point as its third argument (aperture_direction on the camera's parts). backproject(camera, 𝐩, 𝔭) is the chief direction; backproject(camera, 𝐩) is the geometric camera's back-projection. With a trailing t̂, the inverse focus distance the caller evaluated (hoisted, once per sensor sample).

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.RadiometricCameraModel — Type
RadiometricCameraModel(geometric, focus, aperture_radius, vignetting)

The radiometric camera as ONE object: the geometric camera π⁻¹ (geometric: any VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera on the sensor's pixels; Θd), the focus surface (focus, a Focus, Θf), the aperture radius r (aperture_radius, metres) and the vignetting V (vignetting, an AbstractVignetting, Θv; NoVignetting when the camera has none). Every function of the camera takes it whole: a photograph is the camera at a pose (camera → world, VisualGeometryToolkitCore's EuclideanMap), [`worldray](@ref)(camera, pose, 𝐩, 𝐚)`.

The image distance is not a parameter: the defocus is the focus surface t̂(𝐩) along each chief ray, and the cos⁴/s′² obliquity is in V.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.RadiometricCameraModel — Method
RadiometricCameraModel(geometric; focus, aperture_radius, vignetting = NoVignetting())

The radiometric camera composed from its geometric camera geometric (a VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera bounded by its image: a BoundedCamera of a CameraModel over any lens, a spline camera included; the camera's field geometric) by adding the three parts a geometric camera lacks:

  • focus: the focus surface, a Focus, or a distance in metres — the surface focused at that distance everywhere (Focus(geometric, distance), a 3 × 3 thin plate spline on the camera's image);
  • aperture_radius: the radius r of the aperture disc, metres;
  • vignetting: an AbstractVignetting on the camera's image; by default the absent one, NoVignetting: V ≡ 1 with no parameters, which a kernel does not multiply by. A field to estimate is stated: a TensorProductVignetting or a RadialVignetting.

The image size is the geometric camera's (extent): it is stated once. The camera's parts are replaced one at a time by with_geometric, with_focus, with_aperture_radius and with_vignetting.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.chief_direction — Method
chief_direction(geometric, 𝐩) -> SVector{3}
chief_direction(camera::RadiometricCameraModel, 𝐩) -> SVector{3}

Unit chief direction d̂(𝐩) = π⁻¹(𝐩)/‖π⁻¹(𝐩)‖ of a sensor position (chief_ray, normalized): the direction along which the focus point 𝔣(𝐩) lies at the distance 1/t̂(𝐩) from the centre of projection. π⁻¹ of a spline camera is a bicubic blend of unit node directions and is not unit (≤ 2e-4 off inside the image circle on real lattices); the parametric models return unit vectors. Dual-generic: when π⁻¹ carries partials the normalisation projects them onto the tangent plane of d̂.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.chief_ray — Method
chief_ray(geometric, 𝐩) -> SVector{3}

The image formation's chief ray of the sensor position 𝐩 ∈ ℐ = [0, W] × [0, H] (COLMAP pixels) through the geometric camera geometric (camera frame: x right, y down, z forward; not necessarily unit): what a geometric camera answers to carry a radiometric camera. Generic in the element type (Float32, ForwardDiff.Dual) and usable in a device kernel (isbits, no throws).

  • Any VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera: its backproject(geometric, 𝐩) (a unit ray; NaN where the lens does not image the point).
  • A SplineLens camera: its backward spline's VALUE at K⁻¹𝐩 (the lattice evaluation, NOT normalized) — linear in the lattice's direction nodes, which a calibration's Jacobian fan-out onto them relies on; a node's norm is a weight, not a gauge.

Only the image formation reads it (chief_direction normalizes it); every other back-projection is backproject. A camera type of another package answers it with a method (it is one of this package's CONTRACT_GENERICS).

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.CONTRACT_GENERICS — Constant
CONTRACT_GENERICS

The generic functions of VisualRadiometryToolkitCore another package may add methods to across a package boundary (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's contract_generic_violations reads this tuple):

  • chief_ray: a geometric camera type of another package answers it to carry a radiometric camera;
  • the camera-frame maps a record holding a radiometric camera's parts forwards to their one implementation: chief_direction, inverse_focus_distance, focus_point, world_ray (the radiometric back-projection such a record answers is VisualGeometryToolkitCore's contract generic backproject, with the aperture point as a third argument);

Focus

The focus surface: the inverse focus distance (inverse_focus_distance, the paper's t̂ = 1/𝔱) along each chief ray, a thin plate spline of VisualGeometryToolkitCore over nodes on the sensor. A Focus is isbits, for a kernel; its solver, a FocusSpline, stays on the host.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.Focus — Type

Inverse-depth focus surface: nodal values t̂_i (= Θ_f) on the TPS grid, the node positions and the cached TPS coefficients (w, a), a = (a_1, a_x, a_y), for these values. Fully isbits — the solver object (FocusSpline: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's spline system) stays host-side (rebuilt by its owner at a parameter update).

Sensor and quadrature

Sensor positions are COLMAP pixels of the image, 𝐩 ∈ [0, W] × [0, H]; Julia's 1-based pixel (u, v) has its centre at sensor_position(u, v). The integrals of the image formation are evaluated by fixed rules: a tensor Gauss–Legendre rule on a pixel's footprint and a polar rule on the aperture disc, in statically sized containers a kernel takes unchanged.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.ApertureQuadrature — Type
ApertureQuadrature{T, NA, NB}

Polar rule over the unit disc {‖â‖ ≤ 1}: Gauss–Legendre in the radial coordinate ρ̃ ∈ [0, 1] (n_ρ nodes) × uniform angles φ (n_φ nodes), NA = n_ρ·n_φ disc nodes. Vignetting

A camera's relative illumination V on the sensor. NoVignetting is the absent one, V ≡ 1. The fields are B-splines on clamped knots, linear in their control points: a tensor-product surface over a sensor box (TensorProductVignetting) or a profile in the radius about a centre (RadialVignetting); LogVignetting reads either as the spline of log V. A field is callable, v(u), and isbits, so a batch of sensor points goes through VisualGeometryToolkitCore's pointwise! on a device; VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.coefficient_jacobian is its row of a least squares in the control points, VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.active_coefficients that row's nonzeros, and VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.pole_regular_basis the radial profiles smooth at their centre. The estimators that fit them are their owners': a radiometric calibration's, a panorama's self-calibration.

A radial profile reads its normalized radius on a chart of the distance from its centre (VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.RADIAL_CHARTS): linear, ρ = d / radius, constant beyond the radius; or projective, ρ = d / (radius + d), ½ at the radius and never beyond the knots.

v = RadialVignetting(12, SVector(2128.0, 1424.0), 1700.0)   # control points, centre and radius (camera pixels); V ≡ 1
w = RadialVignetting(15, SVector(2128.0, 1424.0), 2560.0; chart = :projective)   # ρ = ½ at the farthest corner
v(SVector(2500.0, 1300.0))                                   # V at a sensor point
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.RADIAL_CHARTS — Constant

The ONE registry of a radial profile's charts, the coordinate a profile is a function of at the distance d from its centre: :linear (ρ = d / radius), :projective (ρ = d / (radius + d), projective_chart) — the normalized radii a fitted RadialVignetting is a spline of — and :equisolid (the field angle θ = 2 asin(d / 2f) of an equisolid lens of focal length f pixels, in radians), the coordinate a measured BinnedRadialProfile is binned in.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.BinnedRadialProfile — Type
BinnedRadialProfile(chart, center, radius, edges, values, counts; focal_length = nothing)

A MEASURED radial profile: per channel, a value per bin of a radial chart's coordinate about center, binned rather than fitted (a flat field averaged over rings of field angle). chart is one of RADIAL_CHARTS, its coordinate at the distance d from center (COLMAP pixels): :linear d / radius, :projective d / (radius + d), :equisolid 2 asin(d / 2f) radians (focal_length = f pixels, required for it and refused for the others); radius is the image circle's (pixels, positive). edges are the B + 1 increasing bin edges in the chart's coordinate, values a channels × B matrix (unitless) and counts the B pixel counts (≥ 0); bin b (1-based) is [edges[b], edges[b + 1]). VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.LogVignetting — Type
LogVignetting(spline::AbstractVignetting)

A vignetting whose B-spline is log V: V(u) = exp(s(u)), s the TensorProductVignetting or RadialVignetting spline read as the logarithm (its control points are log V's). Positive by construction, and linear in its control points in log, so a fit in log V is a linear least squares (PanoramaStitching's self-calibration). Everything but the exponential is the spline's own: its knots, degree, centre, support, pole_regular_basis (V′(0) = 0 exactly when s′(0) = 0) and log_spline. Its coefficient_jacobian is ∂V/∂P = V·∂s/∂P, and active_coefficients that row's nonzeros (log_spline(v)'s are ∂ log V/∂P). Callable, isbits: it runs in pointwise! on a device.

source
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.NoVignetting — Type
NoVignetting()

The absent vignetting: V ≡ 1 everywhere on the sensor. It has no parameters, so it adds no coordinates to a calibration and no columns to a Jacobian, and it is the default vignetting of a radiometric camera: a camera that is not given one attenuates nothing.

It is a singleton that compiles away. Called at a sensor point, v(u) (and v(u, f) with the point's ray), it returns true, the multiplicative identity of every number type: true * x === x for a float, a dual number or a quantity, so a kernel that multiplies by the value is compiled to one that does not multiply. A kernel can also take a method for ::NoVignetting and skip the factor altogether.

The protocol of the fields answers for it with nothing: it has no control points (n_control_points(v) == 0, an empty coefficient_jacobian), so parameter_vector is empty, parameter_names is (), and with_parameters takes the empty vector and returns it unchanged.

Examples

julia> using StaticArrays: SVector

julia> v = NoVignetting();

julia> v(SVector(1200.0, 800.0))
true

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.RadialVignetting — Type
RadialVignetting(n, center, radius; degree = 3, T = Float64, chart = :linear)
RadialVignetting(knots, center, radius, P; degree, chart = :linear)

The radial B-spline vignetting profile: n control points of degree degree over a normalized radius ρ ∈ [0, 1] of the distance d = ‖u − center‖ (center and radius in COLMAP pixels, plain or px; a length is a DimensionError, a radius that is not positive an ArgumentError), P ≡ 1 (V ≡ 1), V(u) = Σᵢ Nᵢ(ρ(u)) Pᵢ. The chart of ρ (RADIAL_CHARTS):

  • :linear: ρ = d / radius on clamped uniform knots, V constant beyond radius;
  • :projective: ρ = d / (radius + d) (projective_chart), ½ at radius and → 1 at infinity, so no sensor point is ever beyond the knots whatever the centre; on the knots projective_knots(n, degree): uniform in the distance up to radius and degree − 1 spans past it at the same pixel spacing, then the span to 1 (n > 2·degree).

Radially symmetric by construction: a field about one centre. pole_regular_basis is the subspace of the profiles that are smooth at the centre (V′(0) = 0 on either chart: dρ/dd = 1 / radius at the centre), and V(center) = P₁ exactly. The second form takes the clamped knot vector itself (checked: n + degree + 1 knots, nondecreasing, clamped, from 0 to 1) and the control points: what a record is rebuilt from. radial_chart is the chart, with_center moves the centre, center_gradient is ∂V/∂center.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.pole_regular_basis — Method
pole_regular_basis(v::RadialVignetting) -> Matrix{Float64}

A basis Z (N × (N − 1)) of the radial profiles that are smooth at the centre, vec(P) = vec(P₀) + Z y: the first two control points tied, P₁ = P₂. A field of revolution V(‖u − c‖) is differentiable at its centre only when its generatrix meets the axis at a right angle, V′(0) = 0 (Piegl & Tiller 1997, §8.5); on clamped knots V′(0) = p/k_{p+2} · (P₂ − P₁) (their eq. 3.7), so the tie is that condition, and the field is then as smooth at the centre as at its knots. Column 1 moves P₁ and P₂ together, column j > 1 moves P_{j+1}.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.projective_knots — Method
projective_knots(n, p) -> SVector{n + p + 1, Float64}

The knots of a radial profile on the projective chart of n control points of degree p: m = n − 2p spans uniform in the distance up to the radius — knots at d = k·r/m, which the chart maps to k / (m + k) — continued PAST the radius at the same pixel spacing, k = 1 … m + p − 1 (k = m is the rim, ρ = ½; p − 1 more beyond it), then the span to 1 for every point farther out, clamped (p + 1 knots at each end). Needs n > 2p (ArgumentError otherwise, naming the minimum 2p + 1). With n = a linear profile's count + p, the pixel spacing up to the radius is the linear profile's (19 for the linear 16, p = 3).

Why the p − 1 knots past the rim (VisualGeometryToolkitCore 0.10.11, T. Harder's analysis of 2026-11-15): with ONE span [½, 1) after the rim — the rule until 0.10.10, m = n − p − 1 — that span is ~25× wider in ρ than the last span on the sensor, so the last basis functions nearly vanish on d ≤ r (peaks [0.667, 0.921, 0.111, 0.001, 0] of the last five for n = 17): coefficients the fitted pixels barely determine. Continued at the pixel spacing, every function that acts on d ≤ r acts there as a uniform B-spline's does (n = 19: peaks [0.667, 0.667, 0.667, 0.186, 0, 0, 0] of the last seven, 16 functions on the fitted pixels).
