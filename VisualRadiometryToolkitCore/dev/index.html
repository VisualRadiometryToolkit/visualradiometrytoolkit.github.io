VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.jl

The core of the Visual Radiometry Toolkit: the types of a radiometric camera. A radiometric camera is a camera of the Visual Geometry Toolkit together with the parts that decide how much light a pixel collects: its vignetting, its aperture, its focus.

Radiometry lifts geometry. This package depends on VisualGeometryToolkitCore and follows its conventions (naming, signatures, frames, units, Jacobians), which are stated once, there; no package of the Visual Geometry Toolkit depends on this one.

The camera model is that of the paper Real Cameras are Radiometric.

What it provides

  • the radiometric camera, RadiometricCameraModel, and its back-projection from the aperture disc;
  • the focus surface (VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.Focus), a thin plate spline over the sensor;
  • the vignetting fields (TensorProductVignetting, RadialVignetting, LogVignetting), the absent vignetting NoVignetting and a measured radial profile (VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.BinnedRadialProfile);
  • the quadrature rules on a pixel and on the aperture (VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.SensorQuadrature, VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.ApertureQuadrature);
  • the scene: an EnvironmentLight of a SphericalHarmonicMap, a Cook–Torrance VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.Material and a TexturedTarget with its printed-edge model and its state.

The public names are descriptive; the symbols of the paper are public aliases of the same functions (The paper's notation).

Installation

pkg> add VisualGeometryToolkitCore VisualRadiometryToolkitCore StaticArrays

Julia 1.13 or later. Every dependency is in the General registry. The package's own registration in General is pending; until it is merged, add takes the repository's URL and the package's directory, https://github.com/VisualRadiometryToolkit/visualradiometrytoolkit.github.io:VisualRadiometryToolkitCore. VisualGeometryToolkitCore defines the geometric camera that a radiometric camera holds.

Example

The example builds a radiometric camera from a pinhole camera, and back-projects a sensor position from a point of the aperture: the ray goes through the focus point of that sensor position.

using VisualGeometryToolkitCore, VisualRadiometryToolkitCore
using StaticArrays: SMatrix, SVector
using LinearAlgebra: norm

# A geometric camera on a 1200 × 800 image: focal length 900 pixels, a pinhole lens.
K = CameraCalibrationMat(SMatrix{3, 3}(900.0, 0, 0, 0, 900.0, 0, 600.0, 400.0, 1))
geometric = BoundedCamera(CameraModel(LogicalIntrinsics(K), Pinhole()), Size2(1200, 800))

# The radiometric camera: focused at 1.5 m, with an aperture of radius 4 mm.
camera = RadiometricCameraModel(geometric; focus = 1.5, aperture_radius = 0.004)
camera.vignetting === NoVignetting()                   # true

𝐩 = SVector(700.0, 300.0)                              # a sensor position (pixels)
𝐚 = SVector(0.002, 0.0, 0.0)                           # a point of the aperture disc (camera frame, metres)
𝐟 = VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.focus_point(camera, 𝐩) # where the rays of 𝐩 meet (camera frame, metres)
norm(𝐟) ≈ 1.5                                          # true
d = backproject(camera, 𝐩, 𝐚)                          # the unit direction of the ray from 𝐚 through 𝐟
d ≈ (𝐟 - 𝐚) / norm(𝐟 - 𝐚)                              # true

A camera that is given no vignetting attenuates nothing, and the absent vignetting costs nothing: it has no parameters, and its value is true, the identity of the product it enters.

julia> using VisualGeometryToolkitCore, VisualRadiometryToolkitCore

julia> using StaticArrays: SVector

julia> v = NoVignetting();

julia> v isa AbstractVignetting
true

julia> v(SVector(1200.0, 800.0)) * 0.25
0.25

julia> parameter_names(v)
()
