API Reference: the scene

The scene's light, a surface's reflectance and a printed planar target. The camera, its parts and the sensor are on the API Reference page.

Lights

The scene's light as a radiometric camera sees it: an EnvironmentLight, radiance from every direction given by a directional map on a sphere of finite radius about a centre, looked up where a ray from the shaded point leaves the sphere (the distant light is its limit as the radius grows). The map is a SphericalHarmonicMap: real spherical harmonics per channel (orthonormal, no Condon–Shortley phase), so the radiance is linear in the coefficients. Everything is isbits and allocation-free, for a kernel; the centre and radius are lengths (ustrip(m, light) for the kernel's plain form).

light = EnvironmentLight(SphericalHarmonicMap(a, 2), SVector(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), 1.5)   # 3 × 9 coefficients, centre, radius
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.radiance(light, x, ω)            # per channel, arriving at x from direction ω
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.EnvironmentLight — Type
EnvironmentLight(map, center, radius)

Radiance from every direction, given by the directional map (a SphericalHarmonicMap) on the sphere of radius about center: a ray from a point x in direction ω reads the map where it leaves that sphere (exit_direction), so nearby points see the light from different directions (the distant, infinite light is its limit as radius → ∞). center and radius are lengths: metres as quantities in a public value, plain numbers in a kernel (ustrip(m, light)); the frame is the owner's.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.SphericalHarmonicMap — Type
SphericalHarmonicMap(coefficients::AbstractMatrix, l_max)

A radiance map on the sphere of directions: C channels (rows) of real spherical-harmonic coefficients up to degree l_max (columns, in sh_index order; C × (l_max + 1)²). Callable: M(ω) is the SVector{C} of the channels at the unit direction ω.

SphericalHarmonicMap(radiance, l_max; channels = 3)

The constant map M ≡ radiance in every channel (only the l = 0 term).

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.exit_direction — Method
exit_direction(light::EnvironmentLight, x, ω) -> SVector{3}

The unit direction from the light's centre to the point where the ray x + μω (μ > 0, ω a unit vector) leaves the light's sphere. For x outside the sphere (a wide view of a board larger than the sphere): a ray whose line misses the sphere is read at the line's closest point to the centre, μ = −(x − c)·ω, with finite derivatives there (not sqrt(max(Δ, 0)), whose derivative at 0 is Inf · 0); one whose line crosses it but points away is read at the crossing nearer to x, behind it (μ < 0).

Materials

A surface's reflectance: a spatially varying Cook–Torrance BSDF, a diffuse term and a GGX microfacet specular term with a dielectric Fresnel factor, whose roughness and index of refraction blend between two endpoints by a texture value. The pieces are scalar functions, generic in the number type (dual numbers, Float32 in a kernel).

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.Material — Type
Material{T}

Θ_m: the roughness endpoints roughness_ink, roughness_paper (the paper's α_ink, α_¬ink), the refractive indices refractive_index_ink, refractive_index_paper (η_ink, η_¬ink, per channel) and the diffuse/specular weight diffuse_weight (μ); the ink ↔ no-ink blend weight of a board point is its texture value (texture_weight). Material(; roughness_ink, roughness_paper, refractive_index_ink, refractive_index_paper, diffuse_weight) builds it from scalars (an index is repeated on the three channels).

source
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.cook_torrance — Method

The Cook–Torrance BSDF, the paper's f_c^CT, for all channels: diffuse_weight × the diffuse term + (1 − diffuse_weight) × the specular term of cook_torrance_terms.

source
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.cook_torrance_terms — Method
cook_torrance_terms(ρ, α, η, ω, ω′, 𝐧_h) -> (diffuse::SVector{3}, specular::SVector{3})

The two terms of f_c^CT without the μ weights: ρ_c/π and F_c D G_2 / (4 cosθ cosθ′), so f_c^CT = μ ⋅ diffuse + (1−μ) ⋅ specular.

Textured targets

A TexturedTarget is a printed planar target as the radiometry sees it: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's posed planar target (the geometry and the design stay there: PosedTarget, PlanarTarget, Canvas, CanvasTexture, Quad, MappedSurface) with its appearance attached — the texture (albedo, the paper's ρ_c), and for a design given as a distance map the printed-edge model (edge_albedo: the ink spread and the print's blur). The target holds its own state, a TargetState: the nodal heights of its warping, the printed edge and the in-plane print error, isbits and replaced by value (with_state, with_h, with_edge, with_inplane), so a kernel keeps the target's arrays on a device and differentiates the state. target_center is the paper's 𝔟; both symbols are public aliases.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.EDGE_NC — Constant
EDGE_NC = 2

The number of printed-edge parameters of a TargetState (edge: the ink spread δ [mm] and the print's blur σ_print [mm], read by edge_albedo).

source
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.HEIGHT_NC — Constant
HEIGHT_NC = 25

The number of nodal heights of the board warping h (TargetState.h): Core's HeightField through a 5 × 5 node grid over the page, heights in world units along the board normal. Three combinations of them (the piston and the two tilts) are a pose's, so a calibration has HEIGHT_NC − 3 free ones.

source
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.INPLANE_NC — Constant
INPLANE_NC = 2

The number of parameters of the in-plane print error of the board (TargetState.inplane): the log-stretch a of Core's Aspect (x by eᵃ, y by e⁻ᵃ; a = 0.001 stretches x by 0.1 % against y) and the shear u of Core's Skew (x ↦ x + u y), both in the warping's placed chart (the page in units of its width, y up).

source
VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.TargetState — Type
TargetState{T}

The state of a TexturedTarget (its field state): the warping h (the HEIGHT_NC nodal heights of the thin plate spline, board_height; all zero = the plane), the printed edge edge = (δ [mm], σ_print [mm]) — the ink spread (ink_spread) and the print's blur (print_blur) — of a target with a distance map (TexturedTarget.sdf, edge_albedo; unused otherwise) and the in-plane print error inplane = (a, u) (inplane_calibration, Core's Aspect ∘ Skew; zero = the design as printed). TargetState() is the plane with no edge parameters, printed as designed. Its parts are replaced by with_h, with_edge and with_inplane, on a state or on the target that holds it.

VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.TexturedTarget — Type
TexturedTarget{T}

A printed planar target as the radiometry sees it: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's posed planar target (its geometry and design: target, whose canvas is the page; the quad its rays meet) with the printed appearance attached — the image's pixels, the optional distance map of the printed layout and its size in mm — and the target's own state (state, a TargetState: its warping, its printed edge, its in-plane print error; the plane, printed as designed, unless stated). VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.is_warped — Method
is_warped(h) -> Bool

Whether the nodal heights h warp the board: a height is nonzero, or seeded (a nonzero partial: a seeded flat board still needs its height partials, the nodal-height mode's). Heights that are zero with zero partials are the plane, whatever their element type: a Jacobian chunk carries the board's heights as constants of its dual type (the chunks never seed them: the height kernel's), and a flat board's rays then take the plane hit, whose partials in the ray are the same derivative — not the height field's Newton and implicit step at every ray (25 thin-plate terms per evaluation: 96 % of a chunk kernel's time on a flat board).
