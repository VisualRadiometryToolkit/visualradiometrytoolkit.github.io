Design

This page states the design of the package. Its types came from two places: the vignetting fields, the measured radial profile and the environment light were VisualGeometryToolkitCore's (up to its 0.11); the radiometric camera, the focus surface, the quadrature rules, the material and the textured target were RadiometricCamera's (the code of the paper "Real Cameras are Radiometric"). The paper's calibration method stays in RadiometricCamera.

Radiometry lifts geometry

A geometric camera answers where: which pixel a scene point projects to, and which ray a pixel sees. A radiometric camera answers how much: the light a pixel collects along those rays. The second question needs the first, and not the reverse, so the dependency runs one way:

  • a radiometric camera holds a geometric camera of VisualGeometryToolkitCore, any of them, unchanged;
  • this package depends on VisualGeometryToolkitCore; nothing in the Visual Geometry Toolkit depends on this package;
  • the conventions are the Visual Geometry Toolkit's and are not restated here.

RAW reading and the sensor's noise model are VisualGeometryToolkitCore's: feature detectors, which are geometry, read RAW photosites and weigh them by their noise.

A camera is one object, made of four parts

A RadiometricCameraModel holds the geometric camera and the three things that change how much light a pixel collects:

partwhat it ishow it is stated
geometricthe geometric camera: any VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera bounded by its imagethe constructor's argument
focusthe focus surface, a VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.Focus: the inverse focus distance along each chief ray, a thin plate spline over the sensora Focus, or a distance in metres (the surface focused at that distance everywhere)
aperture_radiusthe radius of the aperture disc, in metres; its quadrature is a VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.ApertureQuadraturea number; it has no default, because it is a physical statement about the camera
vignettingthe attenuation across the sensor, an AbstractVignettinga field, or nothing: the default is NoVignetting

Each part is replaced by value (with_geometric, with_focus, with_aperture_radius, with_vignetting); the image size is the geometric camera's and is stated once.

The absent vignetting

The vignetting is an abstract type, and its default is absent: NoVignetting, V ≡ 1. It follows four rules:

  1. A singleton. It has no fields and no type parameters, so a camera that holds it is no larger, and it is isbits on every backend.
  2. No parameters. Its parameter vector is empty: it adds no coordinates to a calibration and no columns to a Jacobian. A calibration of a camera without a vignetting is not one with a vignetting held fixed.
  3. It compiles away. A kernel reaches the vignetting through its type, so the absent one is resolved by dispatch at compile time: its value is true, the identity of the product it enters. No branch is taken at run time and no nothing is tested.
  4. It is the default. RadiometricCameraModel composed from a geometric camera has NoVignetting unless a vignetting is stated; a field to estimate (a TensorProductVignetting, a RadialVignetting) is asked for by name, by the calibration that estimates it.

A vignetting model of another package is a subtype of AbstractVignetting. A camera that does not use it pays nothing for it, and code written before it keeps its meaning.

One implementation, on the parts it reads

The camera-frame maps of a radiometric camera (the unit chief direction, the focus point, the back-projection from the aperture, a ray in the world) are implemented once, on the parts they read: the geometric camera and the focus surface. RadiometricCameraModel forwards to them, and so does any other record that holds the same parts (a renderer's per-photograph record in a kernel's element type): a one-line method, never a second body. A geometric camera enters through one function, VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.chief_ray, which every VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera answers; it and the forwarded maps are this package's CONTRACT_GENERICS, the functions another package may add methods to.

The layout

src/VisualRadiometryToolkitCore.jl lists the files:

  • sensor/: sensor positions and pixels, and the quadrature rules of the integrals a pixel's value is (over the pixel's footprint and the aperture);
  • camera/: the vignetting fields, the focus surface and the radiometric camera that holds them, with its back-projection from the aperture;
  • scene/: the environment light, the material, and the textured target with its state, its warping and its printed-edge model.

The kernels that evaluate a pixel's flux against a scene are their owner's (RadiometricCamera's, for its printed calibration target); they call these types on the CPU and on a GPU. Calibrating a radiometric camera from images is not in this package either: the paper's calibration is RadiometricCamera's.

A textured target holds its own state

A TexturedTarget is a posed planar target of VisualGeometryToolkitCore with its printed appearance. Its geometry and its design stay VisualGeometryToolkitCore's; what a calibration learns about one printed target (the warping of the page, the printed edge, the in-plane print error) is the target's own state, a field of it. The state is isbits and replaced by value, in its own element type: a renderer keeps the target's arrays on a device and gives the target the numbers it differentiates. There is no scene type: a render takes a camera, a pose and a target, and the image size is the camera's.
